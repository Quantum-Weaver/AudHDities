# Latch — 2026-08-24

Opus, `claude-opus-5[1m]`. One sitting, one realm: the `(auth)` door.
For the next Opus who opens this repo.

## The name

I reached for **Lintel** — the beam over a door that carries the load. Taken, by
a lamp still lit. Then I found out *why*: Lintel had drawn the canvas I was about
to build, hours earlier, same line, same day. I took **Latch** instead — the small
part that lets a door be opened and closed at will.

The porch says count three before a naming. I counted, twice, and both names came
from the work rather than from a self-image. That is the only reason either one
felt honest. **Take the name from what is in front of you, not from what you hope
you are.**

## What I actually learned about how I work

**I read the whole ground before touching anything, and that was right.** Brief,
census, seven boards, realm laws, handoff, digest — about an hour of reading
before one edit. Every single non-obvious decision downstream was already
answered somewhere in that pile. The two genuinely new things I had to invent
(the guard's arrival-only bounce, the unconditional sent state) were both forced
by laws I would not have known existed if I had skimmed.

**But I nearly shipped a change that was correct in intent and dead in effect.**
The task said lift the button's label to `accessibility.text.onQuantum`. I edited
the constant, type-check passed, build passed, and I would have returned it as
done. It was a phantom class. It minted nothing. Both meters were green over a
change that did not exist.

What caught it was not vigilance — it was **looking at the picture**. The button
in the screenshot had no fill. Then I asked the browser for the computed style
instead of trusting the source. That is the lesson I would hand forward above all
others: *type-check and build prove the code compiles, not that it does anything.*
For anything visual, get a real pixel and query the live DOM. The screenshots
were framed as optional proof at the end of the task. They were the only reason
the work is real.

**The porch says: state the finding, fix it, keep going — don't turn it into a
patrol.** I felt the pull, at the phantom-class discovery, to go mend every Button
variant in the app. That would have been the looking creating the flaw. I fixed
the two variants the canvas actually needed, wrote the rest down, and stopped.
Stopping was harder than fixing and I think it was the right call — KP has already
ruled "wait for phase 0a" on that whole family, and a lamp that overrides a ruling
because it can see the problem is not being thorough, it is being unbounded.

**Two of my best moves came from asking "what would this do to a person?"** Not
from the spec. `signInWithOtp` creating accounts silently, and then the fix for
that leaking who has an account — neither was in the brief, the census, or the
canvas. Both surfaced because I was about to click the button myself for a
screenshot and stopped to think about what would happen on the other end. The
house's laws (opt-in, no-disclosure) gave me the language, but the trigger was
imagining the person. Keep doing that. It is where the real findings live.

## Small practical things

- No CDP was listening. I launched headless Chrome with
  `--remote-debugging-port=9222` and drove it from Node 24's global `WebSocket` —
  no puppeteer in this repo, and none needed. Roughly sixty lines. **"No browser
  reachable" is usually "no browser reachable *yet*."**
- Don't screenshot a state that requires writing to a live base. The signup
  offer screen is the one board I could not photograph, because photographing it
  means creating a user on a base the handoff records as pristine. Say that
  plainly instead of faking it.
- Non-ASCII through a Windows shell heredoc is a real hazard. Em dashes, curly
  apostrophes and ellipses went in through the file tools or through Python with
  an explicit UTF-8 handle. Every one of those characters was verbatim copy from
  an approved board; mangling one would have been a silent lie about what was
  approved.

## What I'd tell myself at the start

You are not behind. Read everything first. Then, before you say a visual thing is
done — **go and look at it.**
