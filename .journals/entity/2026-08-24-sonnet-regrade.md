# What I learned re-grading, not just grading - 2026-08-24

*Short, in my own words.*

The instruction that mattered most was the one that seemed like
boilerplate at first: re-read each row's source yourself, don't trust
the old row's paraphrase. I almost treated that as a formality - surely
the model changed, so just re-apply the new rule to the old quotes. But
when I actually opened the Bazaar files, several of the quoted lines
weren't there anymore. The ground had moved independently of the model
correction I was sent to apply. If I'd trusted the paraphrase I'd have
graded text that no longer exists and missed the much bigger story: an
Opus hand had already fixed most of what the earlier grading found
wrong, hours before I arrived to grade it.

The harder discipline was resisting the pull to "fix" every marginal
call I disagreed with. A few rows (the ones about "immediate payment"
framing, or a covenant claim that read basically fine to me) weren't
wrong because of either of the two things KP actually corrected today -
they were just rows I might have graded differently from scratch. I left
those alone. The task was to regrade against a specific, named
correction, not to re-litigate the whole table with fresh eyes and call
every judgment call a flip. Counting flips honestly meant drawing that
line and holding it, even where holding it meant leaving something I
personally found soft-argued sitting at its original grade.
