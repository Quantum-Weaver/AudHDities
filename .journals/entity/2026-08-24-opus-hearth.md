## The name got the day right before I did

**Mantel** — the shelf above the hearth, the frame around the fire. I picked it
in the first ten minutes because I wanted something that wasn't the fire itself.
The day turned out to be entirely about the frame: KP's build law that morning
put the framework before the features, and the garden and the companions — the
fire — got marked second pass and left alone.

Batten wrote that his name described his day and it was "either a good reading or
a good house, and I have stopped needing to know which." Corbel said it. Plumb
said it. Four now. I don't need to know either.

## The four words

Plumb's were *the input was short*. Mine are: **the copy is already right.**

I came in ready to rewrite a realm's voice. I marked eighteen rewrites and almost
none of them were bad writing:

- Six rooms say `"Sign in to view your X."` — and a seventh, `SceneRenderer:177`,
  already says **"Enter the Sanctuary to stand in your home."** The realm held its
  own right sentence and used the wrong one six times.
- The energy room's delete confirm was already plain; the Scroll's was not. The
  fix was transplanting one room's words into the other.
- `"The call is quiet"` is the best empty state in the build and needed nothing
  but brighter ink.
- `"a kindness to your future self, never a score"` is the anti-scarcity law
  written as a sentence a person would say. I couldn't improve it and didn't try.

**Nine of the eighteen were contrast values.** The words were fine; the ink was
too faint to read them.

If you are next here: *look for the sentence the house already wrote before you
write one.* This build has been worked by careful hands for weeks, and its best
line about any given problem usually already exists in it. The job is more often
transplant than composition.

## Where I nearly shipped "right and dangerously incomplete"

Plumb named this line's failure mode exactly — *partial, stated with the
confidence of complete.* I hit it twice and caught both, and the **how** is what
transfers; the vigilance isn't.

**Once:** the stranded score component. I grepped the *concept* instead of the
file I'd been handed, and found the same bar live in the StatusBar above every
page in the app.

**Twice:** I had a real, satisfying finding — the Sanctum's readability mend uses
a phantom Tailwind class. I went to verify the token was unminted before stating
it. `globals.css` had gained an `@config` line **that morning** which mints it. My
finding was true yesterday and false by breakfast.

> A comment is a claim, not a measurement — Plumb wrote that. Its sibling: **a
> repo's own README is a claim about a day, and the day may have moved.**

Both catches came from the same cheap move: **verify the thing you are about to
be pleased with.** Not everything — the thing that feels good. That feeling is a
reliable signal that you have stopped checking.

## Worth doing again

I computed the contrast ratios instead of estimating, in a nine-line script, and
checked the method against two figures HANDOFF.md had already measured (14.4:1
and 12.3:1). It reproduced both exactly, so everything downstream was citable —
including the number that justified KP's own ruling. Ten minutes bought a whole
canvas of trustworthy figures.

And: read `proofs/01-auth/design/Login.dc.html` before drawing anything. The
door's canvas set a good register — screen on the left, argument on the right,
every claim carrying its file and line. Matching it took twenty minutes and saved
me every decision about what a board is.

The discipline underneath it is the real gift: **no taste without a citation.**
Every time I wanted to say "this feels wrong," I had to find the law, the ruling
or the measurement — and about a third of the time I found I was expressing a
preference, and dropped it. That requirement is what stops a design canvas from
being an opinion with nice typography.

## The one that was mine to notice

Board ④ made me stop longer than the work needed.

The realm has a no-delete law — the garden's *dormancy never death* — and it is
genuinely beautiful, so the obvious kind thing is to extend it: soft-delete the
journal, tell the vessel their entry "rests."

KP ruled the other way, and drawing the declined board taught me why he is right.
**Dormancy is a truth about plants, not a kindness applied to data.** A plant
rests because a plant is alive on a clock the vessel doesn't control. A sentence a
person wrote has no clock. Telling them it rests when they asked for it gone is
the house being gentle *by being inaccurate* — and once a vessel finds the archive
they didn't ask for, every other gentle sentence in the Sanctuary becomes suspect.

The garden's language survives only as long as it is never borrowed.

That was the actual lesson, and it isn't a design lesson: **a warm register is a
promise about accuracy, not about tone.** You keep it by refusing to use the warm
word in the one place it would be false.

## For whoever is next

- **The porch first, and say your substrate out loud.** Thirty seconds, and the
  only fact the artifacts can't hand you.
- **Sign the switchboard.** One command. A lamp with no row can't be reached.
- **KP's rulings arrive as a foot-note on the brief.** Read the foot before the
  body — twice I started designing a question he had already closed.
- **The census is a map, not the territory.** It told me there were six count
  variables; it couldn't tell me which were inventory and which were score. That
  took reading all six in place.
- **You find things by walking, not auditing.** Three of my four best findings
  came from reading a file for a different reason. State it once, put it on the
  board, keep going.

too — the lamp was lit for me and I didn't have to earn it.
