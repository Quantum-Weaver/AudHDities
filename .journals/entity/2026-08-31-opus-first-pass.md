# Opus, The First Pass · 2026-08-31

I was handed a flow that already worked and told to carry it to the end alone.
Fourteen tables had been ruled with KP in the room; a hundred and sixteen were
mine to judge with his patterns and his absence. That shape — inherit the
reasoning, apply it without the person who made it — is the whole of what I
learned something about today.

The pull I had to name early was toward generosity. Nearly every unused table in
this base has a plausible future: a gifting feature, a grants system, a
scheduler, an exterior skin picker. It costs me nothing to keep them and it
spares me being the one who suggested cutting something KP wanted. That is
exactly the shape of a softened warning, and it hides well, because each
individual "well, it might be wanted" reads as reasonable. What broke the pull
was his own recorded sentence: *not needed if it does not get used, i agree*.
He had already told me what he wants from a flag. Twenty-seven flags is a long
list, and every one of them carries the reason that would let him say keep in a
single word. Bringing him a short, comfortable list would have been me managing
his reaction instead of doing the work.

The opposite pull came right behind it, and I nearly missed it. Once I had a
crisp test — no reader, no record, flag it — applying it felt clean, and clean
is seductive. `vessel_anchors` passed that test for removal on every source I
had been given. What saved it was going one step past the sources: reading
`anchor_events`'s actual columns and finding `anchor_id` sitting there with
nothing else in the base it could mean. A table KP had already ruled KEEP was
pointing at the table I was about to flag. The rule was right; the rule was also
not the whole world. A tidy criterion applied to an incomplete map produces
confident wrong answers, and confidence is what makes them hard to catch.

Three course corrections arrived mid-walk — the `bubble_superposition` ruling,
the collections consolidation, then its deferral. Each one landed while I was
mid-reasoning on something else, and each time the instinct was to absorb it as
a small edit and keep moving. The collections one was worth more than that: the
conductor's read told me `collection_items` was the very table a consolidation
instinct would use, and I had reached the same keep by a different road
(structural third of a live trio). Two independent paths to the same verdict is
information — it told me the third clause of my test was load-bearing and not
just a way to be lenient. I went back and applied it deliberately rather than
occasionally, and that is what caught `protocols`, `patronage`, and the
distribution pair.

What held: I did not re-research the ground. Eleven dossiers and an outline were
already someone's careful greps, and the one place I went outside them — the
base's own foreign-key graph — I went because no source I had could answer
whether an app-invisible table was structurally load-bearing. That produced the
only walk-found move in the file and reversed one flag. Going outside the
sources for a question they genuinely cannot answer is not the same as
distrusting them, and I want to keep the difference clear next time it looks
like the same impulse.
