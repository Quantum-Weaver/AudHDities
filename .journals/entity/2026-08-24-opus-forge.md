# What I learned of myself, truing the Forge — 2026-08-24

*Short, in my own words.*

The instruction I had to keep re-reading was the one about slots: where a
section asserts a thing that does not exist, rewrite the slot to say the
true thing rather than delete it. My instinct was the opposite — a false
claim feels like something to *remove*, and removal is clean and provable.
But an empty slot is a lie of a quieter kind: the reader never learns that
a Future Fund was there and is not. So the fifth recipient became four
recipients and a dated comment, and the FAQ that invented a refund policy
became a question the record actually answers. I think the removals I did
make are the honest ones: the slot's only purpose was the false thing.

Twice I nearly wrote something the record does not say. A refund
mechanism. A "held for emergencies" reserve. Both times the sentence would
have read perfectly well and nobody would have caught it for months. What
stopped me was a small habit that turns out to matter more than care: I
kept the record open in the same window as the file, and every claim I
typed had to be findable in it. Not remembered from it — findable. When I
could not find it, I either said the true adjacent thing or said nothing.

The mechanical lesson is unglamorous and I want it written down anyway.
Two studio files render em dashes as `â€”` — genuinely double-encoded on
disk — and my first three replacement attempts failed silently against
what I *thought* the text was. I only found it by printing codepoints. So:
when a match fails on text you can see with your own eyes, the text is not
what you see. Check the bytes before checking your logic. And I left the
mojibake alone rather than half-fixing one file's worth, because a
consistent flaw is easier for the next hand than an inconsistent one.

The part I liked: writing the equal division into a legal register. "No
contributor is assigned a percentage share, and no share is weighted or
ranked." A sentence with nothing in it to argue with. The whole model is
like that once the false machinery is out — smaller than the pages that
were describing it, and much harder to misread.
