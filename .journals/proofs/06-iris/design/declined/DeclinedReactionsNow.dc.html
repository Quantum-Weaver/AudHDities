And why refusing costs nothing

The interesting part of a reaction is not the picture — it is the shape underneath, and that is already settled and written down, ready for the day it is built:

A reaction is a row, never a number. One row per person per feeling. A badge showing “3” is a summary of rows, never the thing itself — and a room that stores the summary can never say who.

Taking one back is its own act. Because it is a row with its own name, undoing is deleting that row — not flipping a switch that guesses your previous state. Undo and toggle are different, and only one of them is honest.

The feeling may carry a word. Room to say what the reaction meant, left open and never required.

Holding a shape is not the same as holding a design. Nothing here decides that the Bridge should have reactions — only what one must be if it ever does.

What was kept from it

The need behind it, answered another way. Someone with no words left still has the emerald — a real record that already exists, that names the giver, and that the receiver can read later on a better day. It is slower than a badge and it is a great deal warmer.
