What was drawn, and set down

The room made whole: an invitation is a real thing with a life — sent, opened, accepted or quietly expired — and the Sanctuary sends the mail itself, so a vessel never has to leave the page to bring someone in.

Why it was declined — three reasons, and only the third is taste

One — it is a schema decision, and it is KP’s. Whether invitations become a stored thing at all is an open question standing at his gate, filed and unanswered. A canvas may draw the two answers; it may not pick one for him.

Two — nothing in this house sends mail. There is a table for mail the Sanctuary sends and there is nothing anywhere that sends any. Drawing a working send would be drawing the smaller half of a much larger unbuilt thing.

Three — the record has a cost worth naming out loud. A list of who you invited, who opened it and who never replied is a social graph about people who are not here — and one of them is a small quiet record of being turned down. In a house whose premise is sovereignty, that is a thing to want deliberately, never a thing to acquire because a page had a space for it.

What the canvas does instead

It draws the smaller true version and says so on the board: a link the vessel carries themselves, sent however they already talk to that person, and nothing remembered. The Sanctuary never learns the address of someone who has not arrived.

The theatre goes with it — the send button that says “Sent!” and sends nothing, and the permanently empty Sent list that reads as a failure of the vessel’s rather than an absence of the house’s.

What was kept from it

The one road worth laying. Even the smallest honest version needs somewhere for the link to land — a page that greets an arrival and says who asked them in. That is on board ⑧ as the single thing to be created, and it is a page rather than a schema, which is why it survived the refusal.
