What a signal actually holds

A name. A description. Its own address. What kind of signal it is. Some tags. A picture, if one was given. How many vessels have answered it. Who sent it, and when.

That list is the whole card. Every element drawn above is one of those nine things — and nothing drawn above is anything else.

What came off the card

The room as it stands prints an emerald count and a comment count on every card. Neither exists anywhere in this house: no column holds them, no table feeds them, so both would always read zero and both would always look like a verdict on the person who wrote it.

The one number kept is the count of vessels who answered — that one the base really keeps, and it is written in words rather than stamped on a badge, because it describes the signal and not its sender.

The card’s door

Today each card is a link to a room that does not exist — a reader who clicks lands nowhere. Either the signal opens into a room of its own, which is a small new page, or the card stops pretending to be a door. Drawn here as a door, because a signal with answers has somewhere to put them.

Waiting, not missing

The quiet Pulse names the three kinds of signal a vessel might send, so the empty room teaches what the full one would hold.
