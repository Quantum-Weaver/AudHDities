How a sigil arrives proposed

🕯️ First Light A vessel is born. Before any word or image — you are here, and that is the whole threshold.

It appears, and it goes. Nothing preceded it — no “one more and you’ll have it”, no shelf of what is coming, no ceremony that has to be dismissed. The house already has the word for this arrival and teaches it in its own third lesson: “a herald announces gently and leaves; it does not buzz, badge, or beg you back.”

Dark sky, never silhouettes

The tempting middle way is refused, twice over. Showing the unearned as greyed shapes feels kinder than hiding them — and E4’s refusal column names “missing-slot silhouettes and the ‘one missing’ state” outright. A silhouette is a hole with a label on it. It performs the subtraction more sharply than a number does.

So the page just ends. Where the unearned would be there is nothing: no grid of grey, no “7 more”, no faint outline, no count anywhere on the page. The vessel cannot tell from this room how many sigils exist, and that is the design, not a gap in it.

The endowed-progress trap, avoided by construction. Nunes & Drèze’s car-wash study is what grounds every display ruling in E4 — a part-filled card makes people finish it. Three sigils and an ending page is not a part-filled card; ten slots with three lit is.

The rarity chips retire with the catalog. Filtering three earned things by rarity is browsing furniture left over from a catalog view. The rarity word stays on the card, because a sigil you already hold cannot be chased — and the glow (RARITY_GLOW :22–28) stays too. That is rarity as shimmer, which is exactly what it is allowed to be.

Sigils arrive unannounced
