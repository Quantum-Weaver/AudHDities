Word find

puzzle_form = word-find

“A category’s words hidden in a grid; the rest of the grid is filler.”

the Grammar’s WordFind molecule, verbatim — resonance-grammar/docs/sql/102-the-molecules.sql:91–94

The content, same table

payload: { rows, cols, grid: [[letter, ...], ...], words: [{ atom_word, atom_id, clue, start: {r, c}, dir }] }

One grid per category_name — the column the atoms already carry, and the one the scramble's generator already filters on. Each hidden word keeps its own link back to canon inside the payload, so the form stays a reference and never a fork.

The mechanic, to the same law

Tap or drag a run of letters. Control of error is the list itself: a run either is a word on it or it is not, and the grid answers without a judge. No wrong state — a run that is not a word simply does not stay lit; no red, no shake. No timer. No score. The list is the extent and it is the ask. Found words stay lit; solved is every word lit; the memory is the same device-local key, and no row is written anywhere. Show me lights one word and costs nothing.

The seed

024-the-dailies-word-find-DRAFT.sql, from a dailies_wordfind_gen.py beside its sibling in the bridge's seeding folder — same pattern: read-only on every base, a review mode that prints every candidate before it writes anything, and a DRAFT for KP's own dashboard hand. No lamp runs it.

Unwritten — his to rule

· the grid's size.

· how many words hide in one.

· whether diagonals and reversals are in.

· whether the list shows the words themselves or their derived definitions — the second makes it half a crossword, and is the more Grammar-shaped of the two.

· which categories — the six warm ones the scramble used, or a wider roster.

Crossword

puzzle_form = cross-word

“Words interlocked at shared letters, each clued by its own definition.”

the Grammar’s CrossWord molecule, verbatim — 102-the-molecules.sql:96–99

The content, same table

payload: { rows, cols, cells: [[null | letter], ...], entries: [{ n, dir, start: {r, c}, answer, clue, atom_word, atom_id }] }

Named on the bus as the study's best idea: molecules crossing at shared atoms — the lattice's own bonds dealt as a puzzle. The crossings are read, not invented: the canon holds 6,728 molecules over 13,449 molecule-to-atom bonds. Also the most work of the three.

The mechanic, to the same law

Type into a cell and the crossing letter fills both entries at once. That is the whole pleasure and the whole proof: a letter that does not agree with its crossing is visible to the solver without being told — Ximenean fairness exactly. No wrong state: no red cell, no shake; a letter that has not agreed has not agreed yet. No timer, no “check puzzle” verdict, no percentage complete. Show me reveals one entry and costs nothing. Solved is the last agreement; the memory is device-local.

The seed

025-the-dailies-crossword-DRAFT.sql, from a dailies_crossword_gen.py. The mask filter is not optional here either: 924 of 2,344 atoms carry their own word inside their own definition, so unfiltered, two clues in five would print their answers.

Unwritten — his to rule

· grid size, and whether it keeps a classic 180° symmetry — the lattice may not oblige.

· whether clues are the derived definitions or written by hand.

· whether an entry may be a molecule's own name, or only an atom.

· how many entries make one puzzle.

· what solution and scrambled hold, since both are not null (022:26–30) and a crossword has neither. A lawful placeholder, or the columns go nullable-by-form. Whatever is ruled, no date column.

Wordoku

puzzle_form = wordoku · sudoku's word-shaped cousin

“A Latin square played with letters, the hidden word reading out on solve.”

the Grammar’s Wordoku molecule, verbatim — 102-the-molecules.sql:108–111. Its own sudoku atom adds the reason: “The constraint is the whole pleasure.”

The content, same table

payload: { n, letters: [ ... ], givens: [[null | letter], ...], solution: [[letter], ...], hidden: { atom_word, atom_id, dir, start: {r, c} } }

Here the row's own atom_word and atom_id earn their keep unchanged: the hidden word is the link back to canon.

The mechanic, to the same law

Place letters so none repeats in a row, a column or a box. Control of error is the constraint itself — a repeat is visible to the solver, so nothing needs to judge it. No wrong state: no red cell, no mistakes: 3 counter, which is a judge wearing a game's clothes. No timer, no difficulty rank. On solve the hidden word reads out along its line — that is the whole reward, and it is not a score.

The thin ground, said plainly

A nine-square wordoku needs a nine-letter word of nine distinct letters, and the corpus holds eleven — blueprint, docstring, synthwave among them: thin, and cold for a comfort game. The escape is a smaller square. A four-square is a true Latin square and a warmer one, and the corpus already gives eighteen four-letter puzzles.

Unwritten — his to rule

· the square's size — four, six or nine.

· how many letters are given.

· what clue holds, since it is not null and a clue to the hidden word would give the grid away before it is solved — withheld until solve, or a clue to something else.

· whether this form waits for the Grammar to grow, or takes the smaller square now.
