The daily cap as a quiet dial

If the bar has to go, a small unlabelled ring could still show the day filling — ambient, wordless, and easy to ignore.

Why it was declined

KP’s ⚛ ruling is five words long and it closes this: “the bubble cap speaks in words, no bar.” A dial is a bar bent into a circle. Removing the digits does not change what it does — it still shows a remainder, continuously, unasked, and it makes the remainder harder to read rather than absent.

E4’s test is “who performs the subtraction”, and the answer here is still the interface, every frame. Making it wordless arguably worsens it: a number can be glanced at once and dismissed; a filling ring is an ambient countdown, which is close cousin to the appointment mechanic the same column refuses.

There was a real case for it — the brief’s own question 3, and E4’s offered-not-ruled boundary case that a vessel’s own chosen cap may speak ambient. A self-set boundary is not scarcity imposed on anyone. But it was offered, not ruled, and then it was ruled the other way, and corpus one outranks corpus two absolutely.

What was kept from it

Everything except the ring. The case was that a vessel should know their own boundary — and board ③ now says all four caps out loud in plain sentences, where before the room enforced them and explained none. The dial would have shown one of them, wordlessly. The panel tells you all four, in words, before you ever meet one. It is a better answer to the same worry, and it is the ruling read literally.
