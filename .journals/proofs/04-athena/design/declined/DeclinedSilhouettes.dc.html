Why it was declined

Twice, independently, which is what settled it. E4’s refusal column names “missing-slot silhouettes and the ‘one missing’ state” by name — and KP’s ⚛ ruling this sitting says “Honors shows only earned sigils”, which a silhouette is not. A greyed slot is still shown.

The mechanism is the point. A silhouette performs the subtraction harder than a number does — “3 of 10” is a fact you can read past; seven dashed rectangles are a shape your eye finishes for you. That is precisely the effect E4’s “shape without slots” finding was written against: the interface may state extent at the ask, never the remainder.

And it breaks the arrival. E4’s synthesis says sigils arrive unannounced; a silhouette is an announcement with a delay on it. Every unearned mark converts a future gift into a debt the vessel already knows they are carrying.

What was kept from it

The worry it came from was real and is answered elsewhere: if the room shows nothing, does a vessel think it is broken? Board ⑥ answers with the sentence the room already had — “The honors await those who walk the path” — which says the shelf is early without ever drawing what is not on it. Waiting is a state; a hole is an accusation.
