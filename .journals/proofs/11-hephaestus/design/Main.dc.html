Our promise

Your data belongs to you. We are temporary stewards at most — and for most of what these apps do, we are not even that, because your data never reaches us. These apps are local-first: they run on your device and keep your information on your device.

What we collect

We collect nothing. We operate no accounts, no servers that receive your content, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking or telemetry, and no third-party SDKs that phone home. We do not know who you are, we cannot see your entries, your library, or your activity, and we have nothing to sell because we hold nothing.

One exception — Resonance Compass

Optional · user-initiated · never in the background

Resonance Compass can, only when you tap to do so, look up album cover art and song lyrics from third-party services:

MusicBrainz and the Cover Art Archive — cover art

LRCLIB — lyrics

When you request this, the app sends only the search terms for that one track — the artist, album, or title. It never sends your library, your journal, your identity, or anything else.

Every other app in the family makes no network requests at all. This block is the shape an exception takes: named app, named service, named payload, named trigger. An app with no exception gets no block.

Your control

Export — you can export your data at any time, from within the app.

Delete / purge — you can erase all app data at any time. When you purge, it is truly gone — the purge is real, not a soft-delete.

Special care for neurodivergent data — the protection is architectural, not a promise.

The three theme states, told honestly

Explicit dark — drawn above, and it is the only state the ground actually has. globals.css sets one body ground (--color-deep-space); there is no prefers-color-scheme query, no data-theme attribute and no dark: variant anywhere in this repo.

Explicit light — drawn above as a PROPOSAL, cosmic tokens only: ground mystical.albedo #E0E0E0, raised mystical.moon #F5F5DC, text mystical.nigredo #0C0F1D, muted void.dark, faint void.base, link quantum.dark. Measured on moon: text 17.2:1, muted 11.5:1, faint 4.7:1, link 5.6:1.

The house link colour does not survive the inversion. neurospark #22D3EE reads 10.5:1 on deepSpace and 1.6:1 on the light ground. Any light theme must swap the link token, not tint it — that is why the light board above is drawn in quantum.dark.

System — today it resolves to dark whatever the reader’s OS asks for, because nothing reads the preference. The base names five themes (display_theme: cosmic_dark, cosmic_light, quantum, sanctuary, high_contrast) and the CSS implements one. Until the light tokens are authored, the room should not offer a control that does nothing.

The 0.3 law survives the inversion. The forge wash at 0.3 over the light ground leaves nigredo at 11.5:1 and void.dark at 7.7:1 — the ground still carries the text, which is the whole point of the law.

How it is built — the shape it inherits

A build-time read of a markdown held in THIS repo, exactly as its two neighbours: privacy/page.tsx:19–22 and terms/page.tsx:18–21 each fs.readFile from docs/. Never a live cross-repo read — a sibling resonance-* repo is not present in a deploy’s build container, only on KP’s own disk.

So docs/privacy-apps/ holds the consolidated markdown, the room parses it with the same parser and dresses it with the same three components. Four of the five existing files are already near-identical; sirens’ is the newest and differs most.

For the build — four things to fix in the shape this room inherits

The date is invented if the markdown says the wrong word. parsePrivacy.ts:40 matches only “Last updated:”; every family PRIVACY.md writes “Effective date:”. With no match the parser returns its hardcoded default at parsePrivacy.ts:28 — March 19, 2026 — and the hero prints it as fact (PrivacyHero.tsx:11,41 carries the same default a second time). A copy-paste into docs/ ships a false effective date and nothing warns. Drawn as a field, never as a date.

The H1 is never read. parsePrivacy.ts:27 initialises title = ‘Privacy Policy’ and :34 guards the H1 branch with !title, which can never be true. The markdown’s own title is discarded — latent on /privacy, and it would silently swallow this document’s title too.

Every section falls to the grey default icon. ParsedPrivacyContent.tsx:23–38 keys its icon map on exact English headings (Information We Collect, Your Rights…). None of the app policy’s headings match — What we collect, Where your data lives, Your control. Either the headings are renamed to the map or the map learns the new ones; the drawing assumes the map learns them.

The hero breathes with no consent. PrivacyHero.tsx:16–17 — two blurred blobs on animate-pulse with delay-700 and no motion-reduce guard; :20–24 and PrivacyFooter.tsx:10–14 add framer-motion entrances with none either. Motion is content and needs consent. Drawn still.

The sensory ten, here

progress saving — nothing to lose; the room holds no state and asks for nothing.

no flashing — two animate-pulse blobs in the inherited hero, unguarded. Drawn still; the wash alone carries the room.

no autoplay — nothing plays.

plain language — the source is already the plainest legal writing in the house: “We collect nothing.” in three words, and “the purge is real, not a soft-delete”. Nothing in the body was rewritten.

obvious next steps — a policy page’s next step is leaving it. Two named ways out at the foot, always visible, never hover-only.

more-info door — the “which apps this covers” strip stands above the policy rather than below it, so a reader who arrived from one app’s store listing learns in one glance whether this page is about their app.

reduced motion — inherited unguarded (above). Drawn as: the wash breathes at the image era’s 47.7s tempo and stills to nothing; the blobs and the entrances go.

Behind the cards, starDust on the forge wash at 0.3 runs 8.6:1 to 9.1:1 across its three stops; the same wash at full strength reads 1.9:1 to 2.3:1. That gap is the 0.3 law in two numbers.

keyboard — two links, one tab order, visible focus rings. No traps, no dialogs, no form on this page.

The refusal column (E4 §6), checked on this board

Urgency · scarcity · counts — none present, none added. No “updated 2 days ago”, no version badge that implies churn.

Legalese as a wall — refused. Nine short sections, each answerable in one read; the longest paragraph on the page is four sentences.
