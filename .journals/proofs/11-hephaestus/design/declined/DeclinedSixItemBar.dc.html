Ten controls plus an auth link, exactly as the room stands today — with Vessel, Playground and Sanctuary added as a fourth, fifth and sixth item rather than replacing anything.

The case for it, honestly

It is what is built, it costs nothing, and every one of the nine is a real realm someone may want. Nine one-tap doors is objectively more reach than four, and a bar is the cheapest reach a site has. There is also a real accessibility argument for it: a visible link is discoverable in a way that a link behind a Map button is not, and screen-reader users in particular pay a cost every time something moves from a landmark into a dialog.

And the bar is calm already. It was designed calm — Navigation.tsx:31 literally says “THE BAR — calm, six realms + three side-doors (unchanged reach)”. Removing five items from something whose own comment claims calm is the kind of change that looks like taste.

The ground that declined it

“i think the navigation should be simplified, since we have the map for the full navigation. [Vessel, Bazaar, Playground, Sanctum (hephaestus) ]”

KP ⚛ 2026-08-24, verbatim · THE AUDHDITIES CONDUCTING PLAN §0. The ruling is not “fewer items” in the abstract — it names the four, and it names why: the map already does the other job.

The reach argument also does not survive contact with the file. The bar’s nine are realms; the map’s ten groups carry forty-one rooms (the-street.ts:42–171). So the bar was never the reach — it was a shortcut to nine of forty-one, chosen by hand, in a second list that the street file was extracted specifically to stop existing (the-street.ts:5–8).

And the second row was the tell. Three 12px items at /40 opacity — 3.2:1, below the floor — is what a bar does when it has more doors than it has room for. A tier of links too faint to read is not reach; it is a list with the difficult part made quiet.

What was kept from it

The accessibility objection is kept whole, and it changed the drawing: the Map stays a real button with aria-haspopup and aria-expanded, Esc folds it, focus returns to the button, and the mobile drawer keeps rendering the entire street as a plain list of links rather than as a dialog. Nothing that was one tap away became two taps and a modal on a phone.

Bazaar is kept exactly as it was — the one item of the six that survives, unchanged in label and href, because it was already right.

And the refusal produced a finding that would not otherwise have surfaced: taking Hearth off the bar exposed that / appears nowhere in the street and that the Header’s wordmark points at /vessel instead (Header.tsx:66). That is on board ② now, with the mend.
