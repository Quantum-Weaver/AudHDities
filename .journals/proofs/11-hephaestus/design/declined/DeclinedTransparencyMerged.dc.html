The case for it, honestly

It is the tidiest answer and it fixes three things at once. One route means one fetch path instead of two, the client-side limit=50 read disappears in favour of the server-side one that already works, and the “Total Volume” card stops being a sum of fifty rows dressed as a lifetime total. Two rooms reading one table under different labels is genuinely the kind of thing that goes wrong later.

And there is a principled version of it: everything is public, so why is there a second room at all? The ledger table is public-readable and the house’s whole claim is radical transparency. On that reading a “council’s working ledger” is a distinction the model does not actually contain.

The ground that declined it

KP ⚛ 2026-08-24, the third waking, ruled by choosing the option: keep the public one; council links to it. Merge and redirect were both on the table, named neutrally in the truth reading, and he took neither. The brief records the shape: “One table, two rooms, no duplicated prose.”

The two rooms answer different questions, and merging them makes the page answer neither well. A public reader wants to know where the money goes — a claim, its evidence, and the shape of the arrangement. A councillor wants to find one entry — a specific fee, on a specific day, out of a specific sale. The first is a page you read once; the second is a page you search. Merged, the aggregates become a preamble a councillor scrolls past every time, and the entry list becomes a wall a public reader has to get through before they reach the explanation.

The street had also already picked this side. the-street.ts:124 lists /transparency under The Council as “The Ledger” — the public page is already the map’s ledger door, and the merge would have made that mapping accidental rather than deliberate.

What was kept from it

Every one of its three fixes, landed on the council room instead of by deleting it. The three stat cards go (they counted a page and called it a ledger), the duplicated covenant prose goes (LedgerHub.tsx:152–158), and the limit is now stated out loud in the one line that says how much of the ledger you are looking at. The merge’s whole argument survives; only the deletion is refused.

The “why is there a second room” question is kept as an open one on board ④ rather than answered by architecture: whose eye the council ledger is for — the council’s or anyone’s — has not been ruled, and the two are different rooms. Unwritten — his to rule.

And the refusal produced two findings. /council/ledger is in no realm’s room list in the street, so it is unreachable from the map and the drawer; and its one inbound in the whole site is the Forge hub’s “The Ledger” card (DocsContent.tsx:81), which sends a documentation reader into a working page instead of the public telling. Both are on board ④ now, with the mends.
