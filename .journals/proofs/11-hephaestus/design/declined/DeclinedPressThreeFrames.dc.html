✕ Declined — /press keeps its empty frames

Drawn, then set down. Kept on the canvas because a design without its refusals is a claim with no reason attached.

What it looked like

Press Kit

“A press kit is not yet prepared”

Media Assets

“No media assets are prepared yet”

Coverage Highlights

“No coverage to show yet”

Logos & Brand

“No logo files are prepared yet” + six real colours

Four sections kept as headed frames, each holding the honest empty line the copy half wrote for it this morning, waiting for real files.

The case for it, honestly

It is the least destructive option, and it is already done — the four empty states were written this same day, they are true, and each of them is well written. Keeping the frames also means the page’s shape is stable: when a kit exists, the cards drop straight back into the section that was always theirs, and no one has to re-derive where a logo pack belongs.

There is a real argument that the frames are information. A journalist scanning a press page learns something from a headed “Media Assets — none yet” that they do not learn from silence: that the house knows what a media pack is and simply has not made one. Silence can read as an oversight; a labelled absence reads as a state.

The ground that declined it

KP ⚛ 2026-08-24, the third waking, ruled by choosing the option: one honest card + the interview form — “the kit/media/logo frames leave until assets exist, and come back around real files by KP’s hand.” That is a ruling about the frames specifically, not just about their contents.

And the ruling is right about the reading. Four consecutive apologies down a single page do not read as four states; they read as one impression, and the impression is that the page failed. Nothing failed — nothing was ever made, which is a completely ordinary thing for a house this age. The four-frame version manages to make a normal absence look like a broken promise, four times.

There is also the frame-as-promise problem. An empty frame with a heading is a commitment that something will fill it, and the house does not make commitments it has not decided on. “A promise would be the same lie in a slower tense” is the copy half’s own sentence about the coverage section (CoverageHighlights.tsx:12–13) — and a heading is a promise in furniture form.

What was kept from it

All four sentences. The one card on board ③ is stitched from them, not written over them: PressKit.tsx:47–51, MediaAssets.tsx:41–46, CoverageHighlights.tsx:48–53 and LogoDownloads.tsx:60–64 each contribute a clause. The writing survives; only the four boxes go.

The “labelled absence beats silence” argument is kept too, and it is why the one card names all four things it does not have by name — kit, media pack, coverage — instead of saying something vague about resources. A reader still learns that the house knows what those are.

And the refusal surfaced the one part of the four that is genuinely true and useful: the six brand colours in LogoDownloads.tsx:23–30, read from the real tokens, with a working copy button. That is now a printed question for KP on board ③ rather than a thing quietly deleted with its frame.
