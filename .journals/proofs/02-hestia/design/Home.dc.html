The build law this board is drawn under

“i magine building the framework for each of the next realms before the features within is a good approach, as some features are cross referenced realm-wise. the game side of the vessel experience, not as vital as a functional bazaar, athena with contents, etc”

What is frame here, and what is second pass

FRAME — the rooms. Found a room, move it earlier or later. Two real buttons, no drag: “the scene never shuffles ITSELF; every re-siting is the dweller’s own tap” (SceneRenderer.tsx:24–26). This is the sovereign-ordering law made a hand, and it holds.

FRAME — the map. Gate ②’s furniture: table or wall, the vessel’s own tap creating their own realm_map row at first choice (:272–292). It is a way between realms, so it is structure, not decoration.

FRAME — the doorways. Home→Studio is the shortest edge, drawn as a sight-line with the neighbour realm’s own beam standing still (SceneDoorway.tsx:51–57). KP’s adjacency law: “all only steps away from the creative studios.”

SECOND PASS — the garden. Built and standing (GardenBed.tsx, 199 lines; REST_DAYS_BY_RARITY 14/21/28 from KP’s care-cadence ruling). Its empty state is drawn above verbatim and nothing is redesigned.

SECOND PASS — keepsakes / collections. The shelf exists; the catalog is real; the rows are not. Drawn as its dignified empty state, untouched.

SECOND PASS — decorations. Rooms already list them (:374–389), and the way they arrive is a cross-realm seam: hermes checkout writes vessel_decorations into the vessel’s room (REALM-BUS.md:354–366). Exactly the cross-reference KP’s build law says to frame first and fill later.

SECOND PASS — companions. Nothing is built. Drawn only as a reserved place, because KP’s ruling fixes what will stand there: “companion clues is supposed to be a gentle reminder about the vessels actual pets in life” (E4 digest §1) — real pets, not invented creatures. That is a fact worth holding a space for and worth not designing today.

The outside of the home — KP’s word, mid-sitting

“community profile is the ‘outside of a vessels home’”

KP ⚛ 2026-08-24, verbatim. It settles a thing this realm never had a place for: the community profile is not a page. It is this home’s exterior — what a visitor sees walking up.

The frame owes it one thing: an outside. Everything drawn on this board is interior — rooms, garden, keepsakes, the map on the table. The scene renderer has no exterior view at all. That is the gap his sentence names, and it is frame-level: a home that can be visited needs a side that faces out.

It explains the wall the realm already has. The home is own-only by RLS design — “a visitor cannot window-peek a hearth” (SceneRenderer.tsx:24–25). With the exterior as the community profile, that law stops being a limitation and becomes the shape: the outside is public, the inside is not. A visitor walks up and sees the house; they do not come in.

And it is the same door from the sky. On board ⑥ the community profile is therefore not a star of its own — the home is its star, and tapping it arrives at the exterior. One thing, one place, reached the same way from both rooms.

Second pass: everything about how it looks. What a visitor sees, what a vessel may put on the outside, how the facade is dressed — all of it sits with the decorating, untouched here. The frame marks that an outside exists and that it is public; it does not decorate it.
