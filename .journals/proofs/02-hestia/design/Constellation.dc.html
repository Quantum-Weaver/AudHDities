A brand-new vessel always lands here: :353–358 returns zero nodes until a timeline event, a sigil or a quest exists. So this is not an edge case, it is the room’s first impression, and today it opens by listing three things the vessel has not done.

The law this board is measured against

“No leaderboards. No completion percentages. No limited-time items that never return … Build for joy. Not for completion. Not for comparison”

L1-13, the Observers’ own words, quoted verbatim on the bus (E2 digest §1, THE ANTI-SCARCITY LAW). Welded in E4 as “no comparison, ever, with anyone” (§1). Not KP’s sentence — the audience’s, and named as such.

Every count in the realm, ruled one at a time

The census found six count variables (§8). The question is not whether a vessel may see how many things they have — it is whether the number is an inventory or a measure of them. An inventory answers “what do I have?” A measure answers “how am I doing?” Only the second one needs a full state, and only the second one can be lost.

Sigils — reads as score. :487–496: a bold numeral under an amber award icon, in a four-across row. A sigil is earned, so its count is an achievement total, and E4 welds the bank’s no-leaderboards law directly onto sets. This is the clearest fail on the board.

Quests — reads as score. :500–509, and the fetch is filtered status=completed (:300). A count of completions with no denominator is a completion percentage with the denominator hidden.

Events — honest, badly dressed. :474–483. Things that happened to a vessel, not things they scored. Arriving is an event. But a bold numeral in a stat row makes “3” feel like a low one. Drawn as a door with a name.

Products / wares — genuinely an inventory. :332, the vessel’s own rows in plutus. “How many things I have for sale” is a fact a maker needs. Kept — but drawn as “Your creations”, because a door to a shelf should say what is on the shelf.

Posts and channels — inventory. :334–335, both scoped created_by. A vessel’s own signals and channels. Honest.

Messages — a number nobody can explain. :333 fetches iris-communications/messages?limit=1 with no owner filter at all, unlike its three siblings, and shows the pagination total as “{n} Messages” orbiting the vessel (:215). Whatever RLS returns becomes a star in a person’s own sky. A count whose meaning cannot be stated is worse than no count — drawn as “Your letters”, scoped like the others.

Unread heralds — honest, and board ⑦ keeps it. An unread count is a queue depth, not a score: it goes down when you engage and cannot be accumulated.

KP’s word, mid-sitting — what the sky is of

“contellation is great, but should also link to the other things a vessel “owns” [collections, works, community profile, creator profile, merchant profile, council house] not sure how or where it all ties into it without overwhelming the screen, open to ideas.”

KP ⚛ 2026-08-24, verbatim, spelling kept. It reframes the room: the sky is not a record of what a vessel has done, it is the map of what they hold. Everything below is PROPOSED — his sentence sets the direction; the shape is drawn plainest and is his to rule.

A star per thing owned. One for each work and each collection actually held — not a tile summarising them, not a count. A vessel with three works has three stars, and each is that work.

One star per profile the vessel actually holds — but not the community one. KP ⚛, same sitting, verbatim: “community profile is the ‘outside of a vessels home’”. So the community profile is not a star and not a page — it is the home’s exterior, what a visitor sees walking up, and its star in this sky is the home itself. That leaves creator and merchant as profile stars, each appearing only once it exists. A profile a vessel does not hold is not in the sky at all: not greyed, not offered, not there.

Dark sky, never a silhouette. The load-bearing one. Nothing unowned is drawn as an outline, a ghost or a locked slot — empty space stays empty space. A silhouette is a completion percentage with a shape around it, and it would smuggle back the exact thing the anti-scarcity law refuses. A dark sky is not an incomplete sky.

The council house is the line, not a star. It joins this vessel’s stars to others’ — belonging drawn as connection rather than as a badge beside them. That is what a constellation actually is: stars are only a shape because something joins them, and a house is the only thing here that reaches past one person.

Tap a star → its room. Work → the Loom · collection → the home · creator/merchant profile → its page · the home star → the home’s own exterior, which is the community profile · house → the Council. It answers his “should also link” literally and makes the sky a way of moving rather than a display case — which is also why it belongs on this canvas at all: ways between rooms are frame, and the collections themselves stay second pass.

And it resolves this board’s own count problem. Everything ruled “score” above — sigils, quests — was a number standing for things. Stars are the things. There is nothing left to total.

An alternate frame, for the half darkness does not cover

Darkness answers the new vessel. It does not answer the prolific one. “Only what is real is drawn” keeps an empty sky calm — but KP’s worry is “without overwhelming the screen”, and the screen gets crowded at the other end: a maker with forty works has forty stars, all real, all earned, every one of them legitimately there.

Drawn beside the proposal, not instead of it: the sky already has orbits. buildConstellation places rings at fixed distances today — events at 160, sigils at 180, quests at 240 (:146, :169, :192). Owned things join as their own ring, and a ring with forty members does not grow outward — it stays one ring, and the ring is the group. Density reads as a brighter, busier band rather than as forty things competing for the eye.

Then there are two taps instead of one: the ring → the room that holds them all, a star → the one thing. A vessel with three works and one with three hundred get the same calm sky and the same two gestures. PROPOSED, as an alternate frame — it composes with everything above and changes none of it.

The star labels carry their counts too

:213–222 builds companion stars labelled `${productCount} Products`, `${messageCount} Messages`, `${postCount} Posts`. So the numbers appear twice — once as tiles, once burning in the sky — and a vessel with nothing yet gets three stars reading “0”. Drawn: the stars keep their names and drop their numerals. A star is a thing that exists; it does not need to say how many of it there are.

What this room gets right

Nobody else is in the sky. Every node is the vessel’s own; there is no other person to be compared to, anywhere in the organ. The anti-comparison law is satisfied structurally, not by copy.

It refused to invent what it lost. Three dated mend-notes in the source (:21–27, :40–44, :313–316) record fields that died in the schema settle — significance_score, earned_reason, quest house — and each says the same thing: degraded honestly rather than faked. A ring that could have been a ranking is ordered by recency instead, because the score it would have ranked by no longer exists. That is the anti-scarcity law being kept by an engineer at 2am, not by a copywriter.

Rarity is shimmer. Mythic and legendary get a colour, never a rank or a position (:179).
