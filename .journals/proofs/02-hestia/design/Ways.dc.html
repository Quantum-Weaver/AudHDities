The law this board is the whole of

“the animal crossing type interior and exterior, gardening and flowers blooming nothing dying, all only steps away from the creative studios.”

KP ⚛, THE IMMERSION VISION given whole (E2 digest §1, L601–607). The bus reads “only steps away” as a distance metric, not a nav tree (§2). So a way between rooms is not a link that exists — it is a link a vessel can see from where they are standing.

The shape the realm actually has

Hub and spoke, and the hub is load-bearing. Five of the seven rooms can be reached from exactly one place — /vessel — and every one of them returns there by name. That is a legitimate adjacency pattern (E2 §2, pattern 4: hub-and-spoke with earned folds) and it is intelligible: every room is two steps from every other, and a vessel never has to remember a path.

It works because the exit is always in the same place — top left, same words, same arrow, in five of six rooms. The consistency is doing the work, not the link count.

Three breaks in the frame

The home is a one-way room. SceneRenderer.tsx has no back link at all. It has doorways out of the realm (the Studio) and deeper into it (the Sanctum) — so the room a vessel is meant to dwell in offers every direction except home. Board ②.

Two rooms have no door from the hub. The Constellation is reachable only from the nav’s user menu (navigation.ts:80); an energy entry is reachable from nowhere at all. A room behind a menu is not a step away, and a room behind a URL is not in the house.

One exit is named for a place it may not go. SanctumContent.tsx:206 is router.back() under the words “Return to Vessel”. Arrive at the Sanctum from the home’s doorway — a real path, SceneRenderer:503 — and the button labelled Vessel returns you to the home. An exit that lies about where it goes is worse than an unnamed one, because a vessel stops trusting the sign. Drawn as a real Link to /vessel; the save at :189 already goes there.

The mend, in four edges

Home → hub. The same back link the other five rooms carry, in the same corner.

Hub → Constellation. One tile in a grid that already holds five. Board ①.

Energy list → entry. Wrap the card that is already built. Board ⑤.

Sanctum → hub, honestly. A Link, not history.

Four edges, no new rooms, no new copy except one tile name taken from the nav. This is what “framework first” looks like in a realm that is already built — the rooms all stand; four of the ways between them do not.

The way out of the realm, and why it is drawn short

Home→Studio is the shortest edge in the house, and it is deliberate: “the creative act must be near the safe place” (RealmMapFurniture.tsx:13–14, carrying KP’s adjacency law). It is drawn as a sight-line — the neighbour realm’s own beam gradient standing still down the left edge of the doorway, “a sliver, not a show” (SceneDoorway.tsx:51–52). The pair’s rule, held jointly on the bus: “visibility is the invitation; movement is a tap on the shoulder.” Nothing pulls; the door is simply visible from inside the room.

And the map, which is the tenth way

The realm map (board ②) is furniture holding ten fixed doors in an order that never shuffles — REALM_MAP_ORDER, first row the shortest edge, hearth beside studio. Its own law: “a layout is a place when its map can be held in the head” (RealmMapFurniture.tsx:28–30). It resolves the bus’s one real tension — positions never shuffle, yet the vessel re-sites things — the way E2b resolved it: stability is a promise to the walker; re-siting is the dweller’s sovereign act (§2). The map’s geometry is fixed; where the map is kept is the vessel’s to choose.

The sensory ten, applied to movement itself

pace control — no route redirects on a timer; no room pushes a vessel onward. The one automatic move in the realm is /dashboard → /vessel, and it is a legacy address finding its room.

obvious next steps — the whole point of the hub. Six named doors, one screen, no submenu.

reduced motion — nothing animates between rooms; the ContinuityBeam is the only thing that crosses, and it stills under the same law as the wash.

plain language — every exit in the realm is “Return to …” plus the room’s own name. No “Back to dashboard”, no breadcrumb trail, no “×”. Kept everywhere.

keyboard — every mended edge is a real <Link>, which is the whole reason to prefer a link over router.back(): a link can be focused, opened in a new tab, and read aloud with its destination.

decompression at the door — E2 §2’s sixth adjacency pattern, and this realm has it without naming it: every room opens with its back link above its title, so the way out is read before the room is.

Second pass, marked on the graph

Nothing on this board is second pass. The ways between rooms are the frame itself — and by KP’s build law they are what a realm owes before any feature inside it: “building the framework for each of the next realms before the features within… as some features are cross referenced realm-wise.” The two cross-realm edges here prove it: hermes’ checkout writes decorations into a hestia room, and the map holds ten doors to realms not yet drawn. Both are edges the frame must already have for the features to land into later.
