KP’s ruling this board embodies

“journal/energy keep the true delete, plain words, one confirming gesture, no guilt”

Ruling 7, briefs/02-hestia-BRIEF.md at his ⚛ “go”, 2026-08-24. It answers the brief’s own question 7 — whether a real delete can stand beside the home’s no-delete law. It can, and this board is the four words of it, checked one at a time.

The ruling, four words, four verdicts

true delete — holds. JournalDetail.tsx:68 is a real DELETE, and the row is gone. This is not the garden and must not borrow its language. A plant goes dormant because a plant is alive on its own clock; a sentence a person wrote is theirs to unwrite, and dormancy-talk over a delete would be the house lying to be gentle.

plain words — fails. :213 reads “This thread will be unwoven forever” and the button is “Unweave”. The Scroll’s weaving idiom is good writing everywhere else in the room; on the one irreversible act in the realm it puts a metaphor between a person and what is about to happen. Rewritten below.

one confirming gesture — holds exactly. Trigger opens a panel in place; the panel confirms. Not a modal, not a typed word, not a second dialog (:210–224).

no guilt — holds. No “are you sure?”, no loss-framing, no consequence spelled at the vessel. “Keep it” is a warm cancel, not a confirmshame. Kept, lowercased only for sentence case.

What the delete looks like today

The trigger has no name. :177–183 is a ghost button holding a bare <Trash2> with no aria-label and no text — and the Edit button beside it (:172–176) is the same. A screen reader hears two unnamed buttons on a page whose whole subject is a person’s own words. Drawn with words on them. Same at EnergyEntryDetail.tsx:89 — board ⑤.

The confirm button is the primary. :219 gives the destructive act variant="primary" — quantum-purple, the same weight as “Enter the Sanctuary” at the door. Kept as drawn, because one confirming gesture should be unmissable; the panel’s own error border carries the warning, and #FFFFFF on quantum.purple measures 4.9:1.

Echoes — second pass, at KP’s word

“second pass with the scroll, is the users ‘echoes’ experience when reading a scroll from the past or even moments after writing it during the capture flow.”

KP ⚛ 2026-08-24, verbatim. Drawn as a reserved place inside the entry and named, and nothing more — the frame shows where it lands, this canvas does not design it.

Two moments, one place. His sentence names both — reading a scroll from the past, and moments after writing it, during the capture flow. So the space belongs to the entry itself, not to a separate room: whether a vessel arrives at it years later or seconds later, they are standing in the same spot.

What the frame owes it now. Only that the entry has room beneath the words and that the capture flow can reach the same view — which is the second reason the compose field becomes a textarea below: a one-line box has no “moments after”, because there is nothing on screen to come back to.

Not drawn, deliberately. An echo is a thing the house says back to a person about their own words. What it says, when, and whether at all is his — and a placeholder here would be a voice invented for the Sanctuary in the one room where the voice is supposed to be the vessel’s.

The Scroll writes in a one-line field

JournalList.tsx:207–212 and JournalEdit.tsx:178–184 both compose “Your Words” in <Input>, which renders a bare <input> (Input.tsx:119). A journal entry — the room subtitled “Your words, your story, your truth” — is typed into a box one line tall, where nothing already written stays in sight. src/components/forging/Textarea.tsx exists, in the same folder, unused here. Drawn as a textarea. Proposed: no ruling names it, and it is the plainest thing.

Anti-scarcity, read honestly

No count anywhere. The Scroll never says how many entries there are, never a streak, never a “days written”. Entries are a list of things, in the order they happened. This room passes the anti-scarcity law without having to try, and it is the model for board ⑥.

Mood is a word, not a score. Eight named moods, no scale, no average, no chart. “Stormy” sits beside “Grateful” at equal weight.

The sensory ten, here

pace control — nothing autosaves, nothing expires, nothing counts the days between entries.

progress saving — the weak row. The compose form lives inside a toggled block (:192): pressing Cancel unmounts it and whatever was typed is gone, with no warning. Drawn: Cancel keeps the draft in place until the entry is added or the vessel clears it themselves.

no flashing — no shake, no flash; “Nothing changed.” (JournalEdit:84) simply appears and rests.

no autoplay — nothing plays.

plain language — “Weave a Thread” and “Reweave the Thread” are the room’s own good idiom and stay. Only the delete leaves it.

obvious next steps — one button, four fields, two of them optional and labelled so.

more-info door — an entry that will not load says “This thread has unraveled.” with the way back beneath it (:111–114) — a dead end with a door in it. Kept verbatim: this one is a loss, and the weaving idiom is exactly right for a loss.

reduced motion — only colour transitions, all carrying transition-colors.

contrast — entry body starDust/80 on surface 8.3:1, good. But the subtitle (:175), the empty-state line (:240) and every date sit at /40 and /30 — 3.1:1 and 2.3:1. The “Tap ‘Weave a Thread’ to begin” instruction (:241) is the least legible text in the room and the only instruction in it.

keyboard — two unnamed icon buttons on the entry (above). Otherwise whole.
