## What the untruths were made of

Almost none of them were lies. They were **placeholders that outlived
their scaffolding** — copy written to describe a thing that was going to
exist, left standing after the plan changed, or after the thing was built
differently. `/enter` was a door someone meant to build. `middleware.ts`
was how routes were going to be guarded; the house built `AuthGuard`
instead and nobody came back to the page that says otherwise. "8
documents" was true when there were eight.

The exception is `/press`, and it is a different species. Six outlets,
six headlines, six pull-quotes, three counters computed off them — all
invented, all `url:"#"`. That is not stale scaffolding; it is furniture
built to look like evidence. I took it out and left "no coverage to show
yet," and deliberately did **not** write "coverage coming soon" in its
place. A promise in the future tense is the same fabrication with a
longer fuse.

## The one that mattered most

auth-flow described a magic-link-only door with no passwords. The door
was rebuilt the same morning, password-primary, with the magic link
beside it at KP's own word — *"sometimes a vessel has not the capacity to
reset a password."* Rewriting that page, the thing I noticed is that the
false version was **kinder-sounding** and the true version is kinder: two
doors, because some days one of them is too much. The truth was already
the better copy. It usually is. The page had just never been told.

## What I'd tell the next hand

When a claim is false, the fix is rarely deletion. Every row here had a
true thing sitting behind the false one, and the true thing fit the same
sentence: "every member shares" → the two rosters, in the hero's own
voice; "creators keep 90%" → the ware's contributors, equally; "0%
creator ownership on most platforms" → the house's own "no equity taken,
no investors," which was already written six sections lower on that same
page. **Look under the false claim before you reach for the delete key —
the honest version is usually already in the building.**

The only claim I could not replace was the one I softened to intent
(auto-save, plain-language summaries). That is the honest shape when
nothing true is behind it yet: say it is intended, say it is not built,
and let it stay uncomfortable until someone builds it.
