## The whole model, in one line

> **90% of everything circulates through the ecosystem. 10% funds the machine.**
> — KP ⚛, 2026-08-12

**Refined 08-24: of that 10%, 30% returns to the residual pool and 70% pays the
machine.** So 7% of every sale is the only money that ever leaves; 93% circulates.
Nothing in the 90% is held back — **both of its destinations are groups, never an
individual** — and the fee's 3% joins the residual pool on every sale, dial or no
dial.

## ① The sale splits once — and the fee splits again

```
                            💰  $100 SALE
                                  │
              ┌───────────────────┴───────────────────┐
              ▼                                       ▼
     ┌─────────────────┐                   ┌─────────────────────┐
     │   10%  ($10)    │                   │     90%  ($90)      │
     │ THE FEE, split: │                   │    THE ECOSYSTEM    │
     │ 30% → the pool  │                   │  split by the       │
     │ 70% → machine   │                   │  RESIDUAL DIAL      │
     └────────┬────────┘                   └──────────┬──────────┘
              │ $3 → residual pool, always            │
              │ $7 → hosting · dev · support · fees   │
              │                   ┌──────────────────┴──────────────────┐
              │                   ▼                                     ▼
              │    ┌──────────────────────────┐        ┌──────────────────────────┐
              │    │  THIS WARE'S             │        │  THE RESIDUAL POOL       │
              │    │  CONTRIBUTORS            │        │                          │
              │    │  divided EQUALLY         │        │  ALL artisans on the     │
              │    │                          │        │  platform, EQUALLY,      │
              │    │  the main artisan is     │        │  at intervals as allowed │
              │    │  ONE of them             │   ┌───▶│                          │
              │    └──────────────────────────┘   │    └──────────────────────────┘
              │      what is left after the       │        the dial's PLEDGE
              └────── pledge — never the pledge ──┘        + $3 from the fee
```

**The residual dial (0–50%) is the main artisan's PLEDGE of this ware's profit to the
residual pool — set per product, default 0.** *KP ⚛, 2026-08-24, verbatim: "redidual
pool , set per product by the main artisan, comes out of the profit after the
platform fee of 10% is taken, default to 0% up to 50%, pays to ALL artisans on the
platform when payouts occur."* At 50% the split is even: half to the hands that made
this thing, half into the commons. **At a lower setting LESS goes to the commons from
the dial; at 0% — the default — nothing does, and the ware's contributors keep the
whole 90%.** The fee's $3 enters the pool either way.

| dial | to the residual pool from the 90% | + from the fee, always | to this ware's contributors |
|---|---|---|---|
| **50%** *(KP's own, on every own ware)* | 45% of the sale | 3% of the sale | 45% of the sale |
| 30% | 27% of the sale | 3% of the sale | 63% of the sale |
| **0%** *(the default)* | nothing | 3% of the sale | **the whole 90%** |

**What the contributors share is what is left after the pledge — never the pledge.**
*KP ⚛, 2026-08-24: "and the amount they share is not the residual pool, it is what is
left after paying into the residual pool."*

**The creator is one of the ware's contributors and takes an equal share** — *KP ⚛,
confirmed 2026-08-12.* There is no separate creator line, no premium, and no
percentage ranking anywhere: **whoever made the thing divides that share evenly, the
creator among them.** *KP ⚛, 2026-08-24, verbatim: "artisan profits per sale are
divided equally per contributor, no setting this value. one contributor, gets all
after the residual pool pledge and their own covenant pledge."* *And, looking at the
public page that still said otherwise, the same day: "to be clear there is no special
ranking for the creator and contributors, divide is equal regardles of role."*

**THE ROSTER — who "ALL artisans" is. RULED, KP ⚛ 2026-08-24, verbatim:** *"ALL
artisans - any vessel ever appearing at least once as either a contributor or
artisan."* So the residual pool's headcount is the set of distinct vessels who have
ever held an artisan profile OR ever stood on any ware's or work's contributor roster
— once in, never out. *(The 08-12 text's "ALL contributors" and KP's "all creators"
name the same roster; this is its definition.)* **And why — KP ⚛, the same minute,
verbatim:** *"basically a second dignity floor. pitch in once, get paid for life"* ·
*"i have been a starving artist too many times, no one deserves to starve"*. The
residual pool is the artisans' dignity floor as the covenant pool is everyone's; the
roster is written so that one contribution, ever, is enough to stand on it.

**Sales trigger the pledges.** *KP ⚛, 2026-08-24: "payouts from sales are what
triggers pledges to fuel pools."* The pledge step happens at the moment a sale pays
its contributors — the residual pledge leaves the 90% there, and each contributor's
covenant pledge leaves their share there. Nothing else in the system triggers a
pledge.

## ② Then covenant applies — to a vessel's share of a sale, and to nothing else

**COVENANT IS NOT A SLICE OF THE SALE. It is a slice of each vessel's own share of a
sale.** Every vessel sets one dial, **0–50%**, per account, in the Sanctum — *"up to
50% of your own profits … from everything you do."* When a sale's share reaches you,
that percentage goes on to the covenant pool at that step.

```
     a sale's share reaches a vessel
              │
              ├──  covenant %  ──▶  COVENANT POOL ──▶ ALL USERS on the platform,
              │                                        EQUALLY, at intervals
              └──  the rest    ──▶  theirs

     a POOL DISTRIBUTION reaches a vessel
              │
              └──  the whole of it  ──▶  theirs.  No dial touches it.
```

### ⛔ THE DISTRIBUTIONS-ARRIVE-WHOLE LAW — KP ⚛, 2026-08-24, verbatim

> *"the payouts/distributions from the pools should not be garnished by the pledges
> we do not take dignity away, nor is it earned."*

A residual payout and a covenant payout land untouched. **No residual pledge and no
covenant pledge is ever taken from either.** The covenant dial fires only on income
that was *earned* — a vessel's share of a sale. A distribution is not earned; it is
dignity, and dignity is not dialled. *(The 08-12 record said the opposite — "a payout
is income; income is dialled; the dialled part becomes someone else's income; that is
why the system circulates rather than settling." Withdrawn. The system circulates
because every sale feeds two pools and both pools pay everyone; it does not need to
tax its own payouts to do so.)* KP's 08-12 sentence *"if contributors have a covenant
pool set up, it comes from this payout amount and step"* stands, read rightly: *this
payout* is the sale's payout to its contributors.

**Who the covenant pool pays, and where the dial lives — KP ⚛, 2026-08-24, verbatim,
spelling kept:** *"covenant pool, set by vessel in sanctum, 0 by default no more than
50% - this  only comes out of my profit at my end. - pays 100% of users on the
audhdities sanctuary, this is the dignity floor. if a user opts in to identify
themselves, they are forever included when payouts from the covenant pool occur."*

So: **every user is the pool's intended recipient — that is the dignity floor.** A
disbursement can only reach a person who has opted in to identify themselves (a
payout needs someone to pay), and **once opted in, a user is included in every
covenant disbursement from then on — forever; never re-asked, never dropped for
inactivity.** The dial itself lives in the Sanctum (`CovenantSpace`, built 2026-08-12:
0–50, default 0, enacted by gesture, on `user_financial.covenant_pool_percent`).
**Where a pledge may show — KP ⚛, 2026-08-24, verbatim:** *"covenant pledge should
not display on vessel face, it should optionally display on outside of home(community
profile)"* — the 08-12 build placed it on the vessel face; that moved the same day,
and the outside of the home is not yet built (hestia's, second pass).

*KP ⚛, 2026-08-24, the contributor's whole income, verbatim: "a contributor gets
their per sale equal share of the artisan profits after the residual pool (if any)
and after their own covenant pool (if any). they also get the residual pool payments
equally distributed among all creators on the platform. and covenant pool payments
when available as distributed."*

## A worked example — one, and it is correct

**A $100 sale of a ware with three contributors (creator among them), residual dial
at 50%, each vessel's covenant at 50%:**

```
$100 sale
├── $10.00 → the fee
│       ├── $3.00 → THE RESIDUAL POOL (30% of the fee, always)
│       └── $7.00 → the machine (70% of the fee) — the only money that leaves
└── $90.00 → the ware's profit
        ├── $45.00 → THE RESIDUAL POOL (the main artisan's 50% pledge)
        │              → this sale puts $48.00 in the pool all told
        └── $45.00 → what is left → this ware's 3 contributors, equally → $15.00 each
                       └── each vessel's covenant (50%) → $7.50 on to the covenant pool
                           each vessel keeps                $7.50
Later, at an interval:
  THE RESIDUAL POOL → every artisan on the platform, equally — arriving WHOLE
  THE COVENANT POOL → every opted-in user, equally — arriving WHOLE
```

**Nobody in this diagram is paid more than anybody beside them.** The creator of the
ware receives $15.00 — the same as the other two.

**The same sale at the DEFAULTS — residual 0%, covenant 0%, one contributor:** $3.00
to the residual pool and $7.00 to the machine from the fee · **$90.00 to the one
contributor, whole** ($30.00 each, were it the example's three). The dials add nothing until raised; the fee's $3 fills the floor
regardless. *(The 08-12 record's inverted dial would have sent the whole $90 to the
pool and $0 to the maker — the reason the first 08-24 correction exists.)*

**KP's own case — one contributor at 50/50:** $3.00 pool + $7.00 machine from the fee
· $45.00 pledged to the residual pool · $45.00 to KP, of which $22.50 goes on to the
covenant pool and $22.50 is theirs.

**The first real test — KP ⚛, 2026-08-24:** *"the lantern, and sirens apps are the
ones i need to include a contributor on, so we get to test this right away with my
wifes profile. i can manually add her in the auth table on supabase dashboard to
enable this setup. i currently have "allow users to sign up" turned off until we are
ready for users."* Lantern at $1.11, two contributors, residual 50%: the fee is
$0.111 — $0.033 to the pool, $0.078 to the machine — the profit $0.999 — $0.4995
pledged, $0.4995 split two ways — and none of it lands on a cent. **The schema and
the code must say where the odd cent goes** (a build question, asked once, never
silently rounded in the platform's favour).

### What a 50/50 vessel actually is

*KP ⚛'s own standing setting is 50% on everything, always:* *"i will be (hopefully
always) placing my covenant pool and all residual pool setting to 50% for my own wares
and works."* Read plainly, that vessel is paid **three ways, all of them equal
shares**: as one of a ware's contributors, as one artisan among all on the platform,
and as one user among all users — **and the second and third arrive whole.** What
50/50 removes is the premium, not the income — and it is the cheapest it will ever be
to commit, because half of nearly-nothing costs almost nothing today and buys the
maximum credibility.

## ③ The intervals — "as allowed" is a viability test, not a calendar

**KP ⚛, 2026-08-12:** *"interval mechanics will be determined on growth and payout
viability, we cannot afford to be making one dollar payouts."*

**The pools accumulate; they do not pay on a fixed clock.** A distribution happens
when the arithmetic makes it worth doing — because **transfer costs are a floor**,
and a payout small enough to be eaten by its own fee is worse than no payout: it
converts the ecosystem's money into a processor's revenue, which is the exact
extraction this whole model exists to refuse.

### ⛔ THE HELD-FUNDS LAW — KP ⚛, verbatim and in his own emphasis

> # **WE DO NOT APPROPRIATE THESE FUNDS FOR OTHER THINGS TO MAKE PROFITS**

**The whole lifecycle, in KP's own words: *"they grow organically and get
disbursed."*** Sales happen, balances rise, disbursement follows. **There is no
third thing the money does in between.**

**An accrued balance is the vessel's money being held, not the platform's money
being timed.** It is never float, never working capital, never a treasury, never
collateral, never an investment, and never a source of yield for this platform.

**Why this law has to be written the moment accrual exists:** the instant a system
credits people and disburses later, it is holding other people's money — and that
float is precisely where payment businesses quietly make their real margin. **A
platform earning on held balances is taking a second cut nobody agreed to**, on top
of the 10%, invisible in every fee table. *The 7% that funds the machine is the only
money that leaves. That sentence is either true or the model is a lie, and the float
is where it would be broken first.*

**What the law requires, structurally:**

1. **Held balances are segregated** from operating funds and are never commingled.
2. **A balance is a LIABILITY on this platform's books, never an asset.** It is owed
   the moment it is credited.
3. **Any yield the holding incidentally produces belongs to the pool it came from**,
   not to operations. *Bank accounts pay interest whether or not anyone wanted them
   to; that interest is the vessels' and is distributed as theirs.*
4. **The holding is visible in the public ledger** — the amount held, and whose it
   is, checkable rather than promised.

*This is the same law as everything else in this house, arriving at the one place it
could be evaded quietly. Consent gates every door: nobody consented to their waiting
money being used.*

### The test is the VESSEL's profit, not the platform's convenience

**KP ⚛, stated as the rule: *"it is disbursed when profitable to the vessels."***

*Read the direction of that carefully — it is the opposite of how platforms usually
set a payout threshold.* A minimum payout is normally the **platform's** cost floor,
imposed on the user. **Here the question asked is whether the DISBURSEMENT LEAVES THE
VESSEL BETTER OFF** — whether what lands in their hands is worth more than what the
transfer took to make. *The vessel is the party the test is run for.*

### The arithmetic — KP ⚛'s own, and it is the whole of it

> **total balance in pool ÷ number of disbursements — as all are equal —
> less cost of transaction**

```
each share  =  ( pool total ÷ recipients )  −  transaction cost
```

**Disburse when that number is worth having.** That is the profitability test made
concrete: *if a share does not survive its own transaction cost, the disbursement
does not leave the vessel better off, so it does not happen yet.*

**THERE IS NO PER-VESSEL ACCRUAL, AND NO BALANCE TABLE.** *(Corrected 08-12 the same
sitting — an earlier pass of this document invented one.)* **The pool IS the
balance.** Nothing is tracked per person between disbursements because **every
person's share is the same number**, computed once at the moment of disbursement by
dividing the pool by the headcount.

**What that buys, and it is worth naming:**

- **No allocation logic, no weighting, no reconciliation.** One division.
- **No advantage to having been here longer.** A vessel present at the moment of
  disbursement receives what every other vessel receives — the equal-shares law all
  the way down, including through time.
- **Nothing can be mis-attributed**, because nothing is attributed.

**The one dynamic to watch, stated rather than discovered:** the covenant pool pays
**all opted-in users** and the residual pool pays **all artisans** (the roster
above), so as the platform grows the divisor grows too. **A pool must therefore grow
faster than its headcount for the cadence to quicken.** *Early on that means long
stretches between disbursements; that is the arithmetic being honest, not the system
failing.*

**And the transaction cost is named, not hidden.** It comes out of the share it pays
for, and it is the only deduction a distribution ever meets — no pledge, no dial.

**Consequence worth stating rather than discovering:** on day one, with few vessels,
the pools will hold small amounts for long stretches. **That is the system working,
not failing.** The alternative — paying out $1.00 at a $0.30 rail cost — would burn
30% of a distribution to hand someone a dollar, and this platform's entire pitch is
that it takes 10% while the industry takes 30–50%.

## Database shape

| Table / column | Was | **Is** |
|---|---|---|
| `wares.residual_pool_percent` · `works.residual_pool_percent` *(`products` was the March name; the live tables are these two — `database.types.ts`)* | % of **platform fee** to contributors *(March)* · % that **stays local** *(08-12, inverted)* | **% of the 90% PLEDGED TO the residual pool**, 0–50, default 0, set per product by the main artisan; what is left stays with this ware's contributors *(08-24)* |
| *(none — a platform constant)* the fee's split | — | **30% of the 10% fee → the residual pool; 70% → the machine.** Fixed, not a column, not a dial; a constant the split code carries and the public ledger shows |
| `user_financial.covenant_pool_percent` *(`profiles.covenant_percent` was the March name)* | *(`residual_pledge_percent`)*, % of sale | **% of this vessel's share of each SALE**, 0–50, default 0, set in the Sanctum — never applied to a pool distribution |
| `community_profiles.covenant_pledge_percent` | — | the vessel's **chosen display** of their pledge, NULL = undisplayed (021, 08-12); shown on the community profile, never the vessel face *(08-24)* |
| `contributions.percent_share` | each contributor's ranked share | **REMOVED.** Equal division needs to know *who*, never *by how much* |
| `ware_participants` · `work_participants` · `artisan_profiles` | who contributed what | **the roster's three sources** — the residual headcount is the distinct vessels across all three, ever; standing confers, nothing else does |
| `residual_payouts.sale_id` · `covenant_payouts.sale_id` | one payout per sale | **`period_id`** — the pools accumulate and pay at intervals |
| *(missing)* covenant **opt-in to identify** | — | **a column the base does not hold yet**: the user's opt-in to be identified for covenant payouts, with forever semantics — set once, never unset by the platform. DRAFT SQL for KP's hand when the payout organ is built |

| *(none needed)* | — | **NO per-vessel balance table.** The pool total is the whole state; a share is computed at disbursement by dividing it by the headcount |

**Seven rules the schema and the split code must enforce:**

1. **The ware's contributor share divides evenly** by headcount — *no weights, no
   roles.*
2. **The creator is enrolled as a contributor to their own ware**, automatically.
3. **A pool distribution is never garnished.** No residual pledge, no covenant pledge
   is taken from a residual payout or a covenant payout. The covenant dial fires only
   on a vessel's share of a sale — at that sale's payout step, which is what triggers
   every pledge in the system.
4. **A pool holds ONE number.** *`pool ÷ recipients − transaction cost`* is computed
   at disbursement, never accrued per person. **Do not build a per-vessel ledger of
   what is owed** — there is nothing to owe individually until the division happens,
   and a per-person balance would reintroduce exactly the attribution this model
   removed.
5. **The covenant recipients are the users who opted in to identify themselves, and
   the opt-in is forever** *(KP ⚛ 2026-08-24)*. The headcount at a covenant
   disbursement is that roster; nobody on it is ever removed by the platform.
6. **The residual dial defaults to 0 and is a pledge** — a ware whose main artisan
   never touched the dial sends nothing from the 90% to the pool and pays its
   contributors the whole 90%. Any surface that renders the split must compute it
   this way round.
7. **Every sale's fee sends 30% of itself to the residual pool** — 3% of the sale,
   always, before any dial is read. The price breakdown shows it as the fee's own
   sub-split, beside the 70% that funds the machine.

## Security & transparency

- **The platform fee is 10%, fixed, always** — and 30% of it returns to the residual
  pool while 70% funds the machine. *The 08-12 pass of this document wrote "there is
  no 30% in this system; any document still saying so is out of date" — that struck a
  true thing, and it is withdrawn. There is no 30% platform FEE; the fee is 10%. The
  30% that is real is the fee's own share returned to the pool.*
- The residual dial belongs to the ware's main artisan, per product; the covenant
  dial to each vessel, in the Sanctum. Both are changeable at any time; the platform
  sets neither.
- Contributor standing is granted by the ware's main artisan *(KP's word, 08-24)*.
- **Distribution is equal by construction — there is no ranking to audit, because
  there is no ranking.** *The advertising stream already states the same law in its
  own words: "ALL participants receive the same. Not a competition."*
- **Distributions arrive whole.** The only deduction a payout ever meets is its own
  transaction cost, named.
- All transactions visible in the public ledger — the fee's split included.
