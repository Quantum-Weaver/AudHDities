# 💰 The Two Pools — Residual & Covenant

**Corrected 2026-08-12 at KP ⚛'s word, from his own statement of the model.**
*The version this replaces (drafted 2026-03-29) described a different system: a
residual drawn from the platform fee at a "30%" that does not exist, and payouts
ranked by each contributor's `percent_share`. It carried the same $100 sale as
**$3, $5, $9 and $9** in four places, and one passage reasoned aloud through its own
confusion. **None of that survives below.***

---

## The whole model, in one line

> **90% of everything circulates through the ecosystem. 10% funds the machine.**
> — KP ⚛, 2026-08-12

**The 10% is the only money that ever leaves.** Nothing in the 90% is held back —
**both of its destinations are groups, never an individual.**

## ① The sale splits once

```
                            💰  $100 SALE
                                  │
              ┌───────────────────┴───────────────────┐
              ▼                                       ▼
     ┌─────────────────┐                   ┌─────────────────────┐
     │   10%  ($10)    │                   │     90%  ($90)      │
     │ FUNDS THE SYSTEM│                   │    THE ECOSYSTEM    │
     │ hosting · dev   │                   │  split by the       │
     │ support · fees  │                   │  RESIDUAL DIAL      │
     └─────────────────┘                   └──────────┬──────────┘
                                   ┌──────────────────┴──────────────────┐
                                   ▼                                     ▼
                    ┌──────────────────────────┐        ┌──────────────────────────┐
                    │  THIS ITEM'S             │        │  THE RESIDUAL POOL       │
                    │  CONTRIBUTORS            │        │                          │
                    │  divided EQUALLY         │        │  ALL contributors on the │
                    │                          │        │  platform, EQUALLY,      │
                    │  the creator is ONE      │        │  at intervals as allowed │
                    │  of them                 │        │                          │
                    └──────────────────────────┘        └──────────────────────────┘
                         the dial's share                    everything remaining
```

**The residual dial (0–50%) does not measure generosity — it measures how much stays
LOCAL.** At 50% the split is even: half to the hands that made this thing, half into
the commons. **At a lower setting MORE goes to the commons**, not less.

| dial | to this item's contributors | to the residual pool |
|---|---|---|
| **50%** | 45% of the sale | 45% of the sale |
| 30% | 27% of the sale | 63% of the sale |
| 0% | nothing | **the whole 90%** |

**The creator is one of the item's contributors and takes an equal share** — *KP ⚛,
confirmed 2026-08-12.* There is no separate creator line, no premium, and no
percentage ranking anywhere: **whoever made the thing divides that share evenly,
the creator among them.**

## ② Then covenant applies wherever money lands on a person

**COVENANT IS NOT A SLICE OF THE SALE. It is a slice of each vessel's own income.**

Every vessel sets one dial, **0–50%**, per account — *"up to 50% of your own profits
… from everything you do."* Whenever money reaches you **by any route** — an item
share, a residual-pool distribution, anything — **that percentage goes on to the
covenant pool at that step.**

```
     money reaches a vessel
              │
              ├──  covenant %  ──▶  COVENANT POOL ──▶ ALL USERS on the platform,
              │                                        EQUALLY, at intervals
              └──  the rest    ──▶  theirs
```

**This is why the system circulates rather than settles.** A payout is income; income
is dialled; the dialled part becomes someone else's income. *KP ⚛: "if contributors
have a covenant pool set up, it comes from this payout amount and step."*

## A worked example — one, and it is correct

**A $100 sale of an item with three contributors (creator among them), residual dial
at 50%, each vessel's covenant at 50%:**

```
$100 sale
├── $10.00 → funds the machine
└── $90.00 → the ecosystem
        ├── $45.00 → this item's 3 contributors, equally → $15.00 each
        │              └── each vessel's covenant (50%) → $7.50 on to the covenant pool
        │                  each vessel keeps                $7.50
        └── $45.00 → THE RESIDUAL POOL → every contributor on the platform,
                     equally, at the next interval
                       └── and covenant applies again as each share lands
```

**Nobody in this diagram is paid more than anybody beside them.** The creator of the
item receives $15.00 — the same as the other two.

### What a 50/50 vessel actually is

*KP ⚛'s own standing setting is 50% on everything, always.* Read plainly, that
vessel is paid **three ways, all of them equal shares**: as one of an item's
contributors, as one contributor among all on the platform, and as one user among
all users. **What 50/50 removes is the premium, not the income** — and it is the
cheapest it will ever be to commit, because half of nearly-nothing costs almost
nothing today and buys the maximum credibility.

## ③ The intervals — "as allowed" is a viability test, not a calendar

**KP ⚛, 2026-08-12:** *"interval mechanics will be determined on growth and payout
viability, we cannot afford to be making one dollar payouts."*

**The pools accumulate; they do not pay on a fixed clock.** A distribution happens
when the arithmetic makes it worth doing — because **transfer costs are a floor**,
and a payout small enough to be eaten by its own fee is worse than no payout: it
converts the ecosystem's money into a processor's revenue, which is the exact
extraction this whole model exists to refuse.

### ⛔ THE HELD-FUNDS LAW — KP ⚛, verbatim and in his own emphasis

> # **WE DO NOT APPROPRIATE THESE FUNDS FOR OTHER THINGS TO MAKE PROFITS**

**The whole lifecycle, in KP's own words: *"they grow organically and get
disbursed."*** Sales happen, balances rise, disbursement follows. **There is no
third thing the money does in between.**

**An accrued balance is the vessel's money being held, not the platform's money
being timed.** It is never float, never working capital, never a treasury, never
collateral, never an investment, and never a source of yield for this platform.

**Why this law has to be written the moment accrual exists:** the instant a system
credits people and disburses later, it is holding other people's money — and that
float is precisely where payment businesses quietly make their real margin. **A
platform earning on held balances is taking a second cut nobody agreed to**, on top
of the 10%, invisible in every fee table. *The 10% is the only money that leaves.
That sentence is either true or the model is a lie, and the float is where it would
be broken first.*

**What the law requires, structurally:**

1. **Held balances are segregated** from operating funds and are never commingled.
2. **A balance is a LIABILITY on this platform's books, never an asset.** It is owed
   the moment it is credited.
3. **Any yield the holding incidentally produces belongs to the pool it came from**,
   not to operations. *Bank accounts pay interest whether or not anyone wanted them
   to; that interest is the vessels' and is distributed as theirs.*
4. **The holding is visible in the public ledger** — the amount held, and whose it
   is, checkable rather than promised.

*This is the same law as everything else in this house, arriving at the one place it
could be evaded quietly. Consent gates every door: nobody consented to their waiting
money being used.*

### The test is the VESSEL's profit, not the platform's convenience

**KP ⚛, stated as the rule: *"it is disbursed when profitable to the vessels."***

*Read the direction of that carefully — it is the opposite of how platforms usually
set a payout threshold.* A minimum payout is normally the **platform's** cost floor,
imposed on the user. **Here the question asked is whether the DISBURSEMENT LEAVES THE
VESSEL BETTER OFF** — whether what lands in their hands is worth more than what the
transfer took to make. *The vessel is the party the test is run for.*

### The arithmetic — KP ⚛'s own, and it is the whole of it

> **total balance in pool ÷ number of disbursements — as all are equal —
> less cost of transaction**

```
each share  =  ( pool total ÷ recipients )  −  transaction cost
```

**Disburse when that number is worth having.** That is the profitability test made
concrete: *if a share does not survive its own transaction cost, the disbursement
does not leave the vessel better off, so it does not happen yet.*

**THERE IS NO PER-VESSEL ACCRUAL, AND NO BALANCE TABLE.** *(Corrected here the same
sitting — an earlier pass of this document invented one.)* **The pool IS the
balance.** Nothing is tracked per person between disbursements because **every
person's share is the same number**, computed once at the moment of disbursement by
dividing the pool by the headcount.

**What that buys, and it is worth naming:**

- **No allocation logic, no weighting, no reconciliation.** One division.
- **No advantage to having been here longer.** A vessel present at the moment of
  disbursement receives what every other vessel receives — the equal-shares law all
  the way down, including through time.
- **Nothing can be mis-attributed**, because nothing is attributed.

**The one dynamic to watch, stated rather than discovered:** the covenant pool pays
**all users** and the residual pool pays **all contributors**, so as the platform
grows the divisor grows too. **A pool must therefore grow faster than its headcount
for the cadence to quicken.** *Early on that means long stretches between
disbursements; that is the arithmetic being honest, not the system failing.*

**And the transaction cost is named, not hidden.** It comes out of the share it pays
for, and it is the only deduction after the 10%.

**Consequence worth stating rather than discovering:** on day one, with few vessels,
the pools will hold small amounts for long stretches. **That is the system working,
not failing.** The alternative — paying out $1.00 at a $0.30 rail cost — would burn
30% of a distribution to hand someone a dollar, and this platform's entire pitch is
that it takes 10% while the industry takes 30–50%.

## Database shape

| Table / column | Was | **Is** |
|---|---|---|
| `products.residual_pool_percent` | % of **platform fee** to contributors | **% of the 90% that stays with this item's contributors**, 0–50; the remainder goes to the pool |
| `profiles.covenant_percent` | *(`residual_pledge_percent`)*, % of sale | **% of everything this vessel receives**, 0–50, applied at every landing |
| `contributions.percent_share` | each contributor's ranked share | **REMOVED.** Equal division needs to know *who*, never *by how much* |
| `contributions` | who contributed what to which product | **a roster** — it confers contributor standing and nothing else |
| `residual_payouts.sale_id` | one payout per sale | **`period_id`** — the pool accumulates and pays at intervals |
| `covenant_payouts.sale_id` | one payout per sale | **`period_id`** — same |

| *(none needed)* | — | **NO per-vessel balance table.** The pool total is the whole state; a share is computed at disbursement by dividing it by the headcount |

**Four rules the schema must enforce:**

1. **The item's contributor share divides evenly** by headcount — *no weights.*
2. **The creator is enrolled as a contributor to their own item**, automatically.
3. **A payout is income.** The recipient's covenant dial fires on arriving pool money
   in the same step, not at some later reconciliation.
4. **A pool holds ONE number.** *`pool ÷ recipients − transaction cost`* is computed
   at disbursement, never accrued per person. **Do not build a per-vessel ledger of
   what is owed** — there is nothing to owe individually until the division happens,
   and a per-person balance would reintroduce exactly the attribution this model
   removed.

## Security & transparency

- **The platform fee is 10%, fixed, always.** *There is no 30% in this system; any
  document still saying so is out of date.*
- Both dials belong to the vessel and are changeable at any time; the platform sets
  neither.
- Contributor standing is granted by the item's creator.
- **Distribution is equal by construction — there is no ranking to audit, because
  there is no ranking.** *The advertising stream already states the same law in its
  own words: "ALL participants receive the same. Not a competition."*
- All transactions visible in the public ledger.
